Global Surface Temperature Anomaly & Atmospheric CO₂
Annual anomaly vs 1951–1980 baseline · 10-year rolling mean · CO₂ at Mauna Loa, Hawaii

What you see
Each bar is one year's global mean surface temperature anomaly, measured against the 1951–1980 average. Warmer tones = greater deviation above baseline. The thin line is a 10-year centred rolling mean, smoothing out year-to-year noise. The gold line (right axis) shows atmospheric CO₂ concentration.

Source
NASA GISS Surface Temperature Analysis (GISTEMP v4). NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, Mauna Loa Observatory.

Global Electricity Generation by Source, 2000–2022
Toggle individual sources · Switch between absolute and percentage views

What you see
A stacked area chart of global electricity generation by energy source. In absolute mode the y-axis shows petawatt-hours (PWh). In percentage mode, each source's share of the total is shown, making the energy transition visible as fossil fuels' share shrinks and renewables grow.

Interaction
Toggle individual sources using the pills below the title. The year-range slider above filters the time window.

Source
IEA World Energy Statistics 2023.

CO₂ Emissions per Capita by Country (2021)
Tonnes per person per year · Click a country to compare

What you see
A choropleth map showing annual fossil CO₂ emissions per person in 2021. Darker reds indicate higher per-capita emissions. The map covers 40 countries representing a wide range of development stages and energy mixes.

Interaction
Click any country to open a comparison panel on the right, which benchmarks it against Ethiopia, Brazil, the world average, and Qatar.

Source
Global Carbon Project; Our World in Data (2022).

Renewable Share in Electricity Generation: 2005 to 2022
Slope chart · Each line is one country · Steeper slope = faster transition

What you see
A slope chart comparing each country's renewable electricity share between 2005 and 2022. The left anchor is 2005, the right is 2022. Steeper upward slopes encode faster transitions. Blue tones indicate large gains; red lines indicate stagnation or decline.

Interaction
Toggle individual countries using the buttons below, or use the presets: All, None, and Big gainers (countries with more than 20 percentage-point gain).

Source
IEA Renewables 2023; Our World in Data.
